# Allied Model Federation

One of Citrate's most interesting long-term public-sector possibilities is not just
single-agency deployment. It is controlled collaboration:

- agencies sharing verified model artifacts instead of raw operational data
- institutions learning from each other without surrendering sovereignty
- allies exchanging approved models, eval results, and provenance trails with clear policy rails

## The opportunity

A public-sector network can be more than a hosting surface.

It can become a provenance layer for:

- signed model weights
- model cards and usage restrictions
- evaluation bundles
- adapter updates
- conformance test results
- revocation notices and trust decisions

That matters in a world where agencies want to move faster on AI without blindly
trusting opaque upstream artifacts.

## Where Citrate is relevant

The existing stack already points in this direction:

- model registry concepts
- learning-cycle and provenance concepts
- private-network deployment paths
- auditable contract and event history

## What has to be solved first

Cross-border or intergovernmental model sharing is not a free-for-all.

It has to respect:

- export controls and EAR restrictions
- classification and compartment boundaries
- sanctions and country-risk constraints
- treaty and procurement obligations
- sector privacy laws and data localization rules
- red-team and abuse review before operational use

## Safe first scope

The first serious version of this should be narrower than the grandest pitch:

- domestic interagency or state-consortium collaboration
- allied or partner-country sharing only where legal review approves it
- unclassified or openly releasable model artifacts first
- no operational use for lethal, coercive, or rights-restricting decisions

## Public message

The right message is not "America is losing, therefore ship everything everywhere."
The right message is:

- the U.S. and its partners need ways to share trustworthy AI artifacts faster
- those ways must preserve provenance, reviewability, sovereignty, and legal control
- open collaboration is only durable when it is cryptographically verifiable and institutionally governable
